<h2 dir="ltr"><span>1. Residential IP: Why It Matters</span></h2>
<p dir="ltr"><span>One of the biggest differences between DPN APP and many traditional VPN services is </span><span>residential IP access</span><span>.</span></p>
<p dir="ltr"><span>Traditional VPNs commonly use shared data-center IPs. When many people use the same IP address, websites may recognize the connection as VPN or proxy traffic.</span></p>
<p dir="ltr"><span>This can sometimes lead to:</span></p>
<ul>
<li dir="ltr">
<p dir="ltr" role="presentation"><span>Repeated CAPTCHAs</span></p>
</li>
<li dir="ltr">
<p dir="ltr" role="presentation"><span>“VPN or proxy detected” messages</span></p>
</li>
<li dir="ltr">
<p dir="ltr" role="presentation"><span>Extra account verification</span></p>
</li>
<li dir="ltr">
<p dir="ltr" role="presentation"><span>Streaming restrictions</span></p>
</li>
<li dir="ltr">
<p dir="ltr" role="presentation"><span>Suspicious login warnings</span></p>
</li>
</ul>
<p dir="ltr"><span>Residential IPs come from residential networks and can look more like ordinary household internet connections.</span></p>
<p dir="ltr"><span>They don't guarantee access to every service, but they can provide a more natural network profile when shared VPN IPs are causing problems. <br><br></span></p>
